Look Book

Structure is settled: Aurora Full — frosted glass, 18px radii, gradient hero, sparkline, floating pill nav. Every column below is that same structure with nothing changed but colour tokens, which is the look × palette split doing exactly what it was designed for. The violet is gone. Four palettes drawn from the hunting world rather than from a fintech dashboard.

Palette 1

Blaze

Safety orange on warm black. The one colour this audience is legally required to wear, so it reads as hunting before a single word is understood.

Loudest. Instant category recognition.

Palette 2

First Light

Pre-dawn plum ground with a rust-to-gold bloom rising from the bottom edge, like sun coming up behind a treeline. The hour they are actually out there.

Most atmospheric. Sells the feeling.

Palette 3

Timber

Moss green into aged brass on a green-black ground. No orange anywhere. Reads like good gear rather than like an app — closer to Filson than to a trading terminal.

Most understated. Widest audience.

Palette 4

Night Vision

Deep red into amber, and almost no blue anywhere on the screen. Long-wavelength light does not bleach rhodopsin, which is the entire reason hunters use red headlamps.

The only one with a functional argument.

The accent is not the weather. Blaze orange is the obvious hunting colour, but this app's signature alert is a freeze — and an orange freeze warning is nonsense. So every palette carries a separate cold semantic token, used for overnight lows and freeze figures, while the accent is reserved for interface chrome: active tabs, armed pills, the hero gradient, the FAB. Keeping those two channels apart is what lets a warm brand colour coexist with cold-weather data.
Your own positioning says this app is not hunting-only. The project CLAUDE.md names four target users: livestock owners, hunters, outdoor workers, and anyone wanting proactive alerts. Blaze commits hardest to hunting and may read oddly to a rancher checking trough freeze risk. Timber and First Light carry outdoor credibility without narrowing the category. If hunting is now the wedge you go to market through, Blaze is correct and the trade is deliberate — worth saying out loud rather than discovering it in a store listing.
Night Vision's argument is physiological, not aesthetic. Rod cells recover dark adaptation far better under long-wavelength light, which is why red headlamps are standard kit for a 4:45am walk in. A phone screen full of blue-white at that hour costs several minutes of night vision. If the app is genuinely opened in the dark before a sit, this palette is the only one of the four that does its user a favour — and none of your reference screenshots had any reason to think about it.
Still open, and both block spec freeze. The brief's Scenario 3 and out-of-scope list still assert that a look change leaves the palette alone and that the three existing palettes stay constant — every candidate here contradicts that, so the brief needs revising and both gates re-running before Phase 1. And a dark default remains the most visible thing you can push, so default-for-everyone versus opt-in is still undecided.
