Look Book

Rounds 1 and 2 were all light-mode navy — conservative variations on the palette already shipping. That was the miss. These three keep Blocked's DNA (tonal fields, solid blocks, zero radius) and take it dark, with monospace carrying the data. Both levers ship free over the air. Blocked is held in the first column for comparison.

R1 Depth & Surface→ R2 Ruled→ R2 Blocked front-runner→ R3 · three dark candidates

Candidate C

Nocturne

Near-black ground, graphite fields, one ice-blue accent. A 200-weight mono numeral at −3 tracking. Restrained and expensive-looking — a premium instrument read at night.

Candidate D

Signal

The alert state stops being a tinted word and becomes a full-bleed saturated field. Accent used structurally at scale, not as a small pop. A 3px rail marks armed rules. Loudest of the three.

Candidate E

Grid

No cards anywhere. A hairline lattice and a signal-amber accent, every figure and label in mono. Telemetry readout rather than app screen. Densest and most technical.

Monospace costs nothing. No expo-font needed — both platforms ship a system mono, reached with Platform.select({android:'monospace', ios:'Menlo'}). That is the single largest "technical instrument" signal available, and it has been sitting unused. These frames use the browser's system mono for the same reason the sans frames use the system sans: it is what will actually render on the device.
This breaks the architecture I committed in the brief, and the brief has to change. Scenario 3 currently asserts that changing the look leaves the palette untouched, and the out-of-scope list says the three existing palettes stay constant. All three candidates here are inherently dark, so those two statements can no longer both hold. The honest fix is that each look carries its own palette family: LOOKS.classic keeps today's three, the new look ships its own, and "Revert to Classic" restores structure and colors — which is what a user means by it anyway. I will revise the brief and re-run both gates once you pick, rather than shipping a contract that contradicts the design.
One real risk in going dark by default. Existing users opened a light app yesterday. A dark default is the most visible change you can ship over the air, and it arrives without warning. Worth deciding whether the new look is the default for everyone or opt-in on first launch after the update. Unchanged from earlier rounds: app/_layout.tsx and UpdateCheckScreen.tsx keep hardcoded brand navy to match the native splash, and radar.tsx line 65 carries a Mapbox-native style prop that migration tooling must skip.
