Look Book

Round 1 was rejected: it built hierarchy out of rounded corners and soft elevation. Both candidates here are square at every edge and carry no shadows at all. They split on one question — whether surfaces separate by hairline or by tone. All three columns below are the same markup; only token values differ.

Round 1 · rejected

Depth & Surface

16px radii, four-level shadow ramp. Depth as the organizing principle — the friendly-native idiom, and the wrong answer here.

Candidate A

Ruled

Near-white ground, 1px hairlines, zero radius, zero shadow. A 250-weight display numeral at −2.4 tracking against 10px/700 labels at +1.5. A 2px accent rail marks state. Reads as a spec sheet or instrument panel: precise, quiet, high information density.

Candidate B

Blocked

Cool slate ground with white fields butted on 1px seams. Surfaces separate by tone, so nothing needs an outline. The alert state becomes a solid navy field rather than a tinted word. Heavier, more assertive, strongest in direct sun.

Square is cheaper than round. Dropping to a single radius of 0 and no shadow ramp removes two whole token categories from the contract — the structural key count falls from 26 to 21, and there is no per-platform shadow/elevation pairing to keep in sync. Still pure OTA: reanimated 4.1.1 and react-native-worklets 0.5.1 are already installed with newArchEnabled on.
The one real risk in going square. Android's own design language is rounded, so square edges read as deliberately non-native — which is the point, but it means the tab bar and hit targets need explicit attention: with no radius and no shadow, a tappable row must be distinguished by tone or rule, or it stops looking tappable. Both candidates handle this, A with a 2px rail and B with a filled field. Worth checking specifically on the device pass.
Unchanged from round 1. app/_layout.tsx and UpdateCheckScreen.tsx keep their hardcoded brand navy to match the native splash. radar.tsx line 65 carries a Mapbox-native style prop that migration tooling must skip, and that screen gets its own device check. These frames render in the OS font because custom typefaces need expo-font, a native module that would force new store binaries.
